Instrumented 3D-Printed Planetary Gearbox

I am designing a printed planetary gearbox with a target reduction near 5:1. The final assembly will measure speed, torque, backlash, temperature, and efficiency.

Project Overview

Project requirements

The design will be checked with calculations, CAD, FEA, and instrumented testing.

In Development

Design goals

  • Create a compact, motor-driven planetary gearbox with replaceable 3D-printed gears.
  • Use metal shafts and planet pins with bearings or bushings inside a printable housing.
  • Calculate gear forces, shaft loads, bearing reactions, and expected output performance.
  • Compare predicted performance with measured speed, torque, backlash, temperature, and efficiency.
Current Progress

Current work

  • Defining the operating requirements and gearbox architecture.
  • Selecting the input, output, and fixed gearbox components.
  • Developing the gear-ratio and force-calculation spreadsheet.
  • Comparing tooth-count combinations and overall gearbox dimensions.
  • Planning the CAD structure, hardware, and measurement setup.

Development Roadmap

Planned work

The concept and preliminary design are in progress; manufacturing, FEA, and physical testing have not started.

In Development

Calculate the loads

Calculate the ratio, pitch diameters, center distances, tooth forces, planet-pin loads, shaft stress, bearing reactions, and expected output.

CAD Model in Progress

Build the CAD assembly

Model the gears, carrier, shafts, housing, bearings, and fasteners, then define fits, backlash, tolerances, drawings, and the bill of materials.

Analysis Pending

Check a critical component

Analyze the selected critical component in ANSYS Mechanical, run a mesh-convergence check, and compare the result with hand calculations.

Manufacturing Planned

Manufacture and assemble

Print the housing and replaceable gears, then install the metal shafts, planet pins, bearings or bushings, and fasteners.

Testing Planned

Measure gearbox performance

Use a motor-driven fixture to measure input and output RPM, reduction ratio, torque, backlash, temperature rise, efficiency, and sustainable load.

Torque = Force × Moment Arm Length
Results After Validation

Compare results and revise

Compare the measurements with the calculations, identify the largest mismatch or failure mode, and revise the affected component.
