Simulation Strategy

Analysis sequence

I separated duct selection from the later thermal test so the outlet candidates could be compared directly.

Start with the duct geometry

I first modeled the fan, duct, aluminum block, heat sink, airflow, and heating together. That setup was too slow for comparing several outlets, so I removed the thermal components and evaluated duct resistance first.

DecisionSelect the ducts before running the complete thermal model.

Calculate each duct's resistance

I prescribed several inlet velocities and recorded the required static pressure. The D90 and rounded 70 × 40 sweeps followed the expected ΔP = KQ² relationship; matched-flow results supplied the comparison coefficients for D115 and 70 × 70.

ResultOne pressure–flow curve for each outlet.

Match each duct to the fan

The imposed-flow simulations show duct resistance, not the airflow the fan will deliver. I intersected each duct curve with the fan P–Q curve to calculate its operating airflow and outlet velocity.

DecisionPrint the rounded 70 × 40 outlet for thermal testing.

Fan–System Interaction

Fan operating point and system resistance

Resistance determines delivered airflow

Each documented CFD sweep produced a duct system curve. Its intersection with the ARCTIC P12 Pro P–Q curve gives the predicted operating airflow.

Shown here4 system curves
Selected flow≈42 CFM70 × 40 case
Selected speed≈7.0 m/sPredicted outlet average
Operating point: ΔPfan(Q) = ΔPduct(Q)

The 70 × 40 mm duct has the steepest resistance curve. The fan therefore operates near 42 CFM, not the 76.9 CFM imposed in the highest-flow CFD case.

Reading the units

CFM is total airflow; m/s is outlet speed. Converting between them requires the outlet area. Pressure converts directly.

Flow → speedm/s = CFM ÷ (2,118.88 × area in m²)
Speed → flowCFM = m/s × area in m² × 2,118.88
Pa → mmH₂OmmH₂O = Pa ÷ 9.80665
mmH₂O → PaPa = mmH₂O × 9.80665
Conclusion

Selected for testing: rounded 70 × 40 mm outlet

It had the highest fan-corrected outlet velocity and matches the 67 × 36 mm heat-sink footprint. Physical testing will compare its temperature response with the straight and 70 × 70 mm ducts.

Test-Fixture Engineering

Heater-block support fixture

The fixture keeps the hot components away from the PLA while supporting the test assembly with minimal material.

Keep the block supported and the center open

Four raised posts carry the M3 fasteners while the open frame keeps the hot block off the PLA and limits print material.

Check the load path under gravity

The static study checks the combined gravity load through the four posts. Low-stress material was removed while the load paths and interfaces stayed intact.

Validate the fabricated fixture

The printed mount carries the aluminum block and heat sink in the test setup. M3 fasteners keep the fixture serviceable between duct tests.
