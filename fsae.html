Current Work

Finalize the tab analysis before machining

The current work is to finalize the tab load cases, factor of safety, and fatigue assumptions while preparing to use the Tormach CNC mill.

Main Constraints

Packaging and manufacturing constraints

Chassis tubes, chain and sprocket clearance, halfshaft position, CV-joint movement, bearing locations, bolt access, assembly sequence, manufacturability, and durability over multiple seasons.

Mapped the drivetrain interfaces

I first traced how the differential, sprockets, chain, bearings, halfshafts, eccentric cams, and chassis connect. This established the mount interfaces and load paths before I changed the geometry.

Stage 03 · Clearance Revision

Revised the differential position for clearance

The first position left limited clearance near the chassis tubes and halfshaft. I moved the differential while preserving the sprocket and chain relationship, then rebuilt the mount geometry around the new position.

Stage 04 · Joint and Fastener Revision

Revised the joint and fasteners

I changed the bolt size, hole geometry, and surrounding mount features to improve clamping and assembly access. The current design uses M8×1.5 class 8.8 hardware; the bolt class sets the allowable preload and tightening torque.

Stage 05 · Statics and Load Path

Calculated the mount reactions

I used the chain force, differential reactions, bearing loads, and cam geometry to calculate the loads transferred into the chassis. Those reactions became the boundary conditions for the left and right mount analyses.

Checked stress, displacement, and safety factor

Observed the drivetrain under dyno load

Stage 08 · Tab CAD, FEA & Fatigue Research

Applying side-specific loads to the mounting tabs

I modeled separate left and right tabs because the calculated reactions differ by side. The current FEA uses those loads while the constraints and factor-of-safety calculation are being finalized. Next, I will use material-specific S-N curves for 7075-T6 aluminum and AISI 4130 steel annealed at 865 °C to estimate fatigue life. The tab dimensions use available nominal stock sizes to avoid custom material cutting and reduce cost.

Left tab load1,047.9 lbfper tab · 2,095.8 lbf tab-pair load
Right tab load496.65 lbfper tab · 993.3 lbf tab-pair load
  • Materials under evaluation: 7075-T6 aluminum and AISI 4130 steel, annealed at 865 °C
  • Fatigue method: material-specific S-N curve evaluation
  • Boundary condition: welded mounting edge fixed
  • Load application: 50% of each tab-pair load applied to the inner semicylindrical bolt-hole surface
  • Chain-force direction: 60° estimate used in the FEA model
